How to play

The core rules every match runs on, start to finish.

1. Setup

Choose a battlefield, then place each hero and their sidekicks on the matching starting spaces (any space marked with a diamond number). Shuffle your hero's unique action deck and draw a hand of five cards. Decide who takes the first turn — the first player breaks ties on any simultaneous effects for the rest of the game.

2. Actions

On your turn you take actions. Most turns you'll use one or more of:

  • Maneuver — draw a card and/or move a fighter up to its move value.
  • Attack — declare a fighter and a legal target, then resolve combat.
  • Scheme — play a scheme (purple) card for its non-combat effect.

You start with a hand of five and can hold up to seven; discard down to the limit at the end of your turn.

3. Movement & zones

Fighters move one connected space at a time along the printed lines, up to their move value — you never have to use the full amount. Two spaces are adjacent when a single line connects them, which is required for melee attacks. A zone is a coloured region of the board; ranged attacks can hit any fighter sharing a zone, not just an adjacent one.

4. Combat sequence

  1. Declare the attack and its target.
  2. Attacker, then defender, each play one card face down.
  3. Reveal both cards simultaneously.
  4. Resolve card effects in timing order, then compare values.

5. Card effect timing

  1. 1. Immediately Resolve text labeled "Immediately" first. Defender resolves their immediate effect, then the attacker resolves theirs. (Note: Effects like Feint cancel opponent card text here, but do not alter base printed card values).
  2. 2. During Combat Resolve text labeled "During Combat" next. Defender's effect goes first, then the attacker's. Card modifications to values (like adding value to an attack or defense) happen in this step.
  3. 3. Combat Math Calculate the combat result. Subtract the defense value from the attack value. Apply damage to the defender's health dial.
  4. 4. After Combat Resolve text labeled "After Combat". Defender's after-combat effect resolves first, followed by the attacker's after-combat effect.
  5. 5. Cleanup Place all played cards into their respective owners' discard piles.

6. Winning

Defeat the opposing hero — reduce their health to zero — to win the game. In 2v2 and Free-For-All games, check the rules variant for your format, since attacking restrictions can differ on the opening turn.

Glossary

Key terms used across hero cards and rulings.

Adjacent
Two spaces connected by a single line. Melee attacks require the attacker and target to be adjacent.
After combat
An AFTER COMBAT effect resolves once damage from the combat has been dealt.
Attack
An action that pits an attack value against a defense value to deal damage. Can be melee or ranged.
Boost
Playing a card face down to add its boost number (top-left) to an attack, or to power a BOOST effect.
Card type
Each card is an attack, a defense, a versatile (either), or a scheme (non-combat) card.
Character card
The card identifying a hero — lists their move value, starting health, and special ability.
Combat
The exchange after an attack: both players play a card face down, reveal, resolve effects by timing, then compare values.
Damage
A successful attack deals damage equal to its value, reducing the target's health.
Defeated
A fighter at zero health is removed from play. Defeat your opponent's hero to win.
Draw
Take the top card of your action deck into your hand.
During combat
Resolves after immediate effects and before attack and defense values are compared.
Errata
Official corrections from Restoration Games that override printed card text. Errata beats card text, which beats general rules.
Exhausted deck
A deck with no cards left. You do not reshuffle — you simply cannot draw until an effect lets you.
Fighter
Any miniature or token you control on the battlefield — your hero plus any sidekicks.
First player
The player who takes the first turn, chosen during setup. Breaks ties on simultaneous effects.
Hand
The cards a player holds. Start with five, hold up to seven, discard down at end of turn.
Health
A fighter's life total, tracked on a health dial. At zero health the fighter is defeated.
Hero
Your primary fighter, with a character card, special ability, and unique deck. Lose if defeated.
Immediately
Resolves the moment combat cards are revealed, before the other timing windows.
Maneuver
Draw a card and move one of your fighters up to its move value — you may do either or both.
Melee attack
An attack that may only target an adjacent fighter.
Move value
The number on a hero card showing the maximum spaces that fighter may maneuver in one turn.
One-way connection
A board connection that may be crossed in one direction only.
Ongoing effect
A static effect that stays in play and applies continuously until it leaves.
Passage
A special board connection (such as a door or teleport) linking spaces that aren't otherwise adjacent.
Placed
A fighter set directly into a space by an effect. Placing ignores normal movement rules.
Push
Forced movement of a fighter caused by an opponent's effect. It still follows the printed lines.
Ranged attack
An attack that may target any fighter in the same zone, not just an adjacent one.
Scheme
Play a scheme (purple) card from your hand to resolve its non-combat effect.
Sidekick
A secondary fighter represented by a token. Can be defeated without ending the game.
Space
A single position on the battlefield. Fighters move between connected spaces along printed lines.
Special ability
The unique rule printed on a hero's character card. Always in effect unless stated otherwise.
Timing
The order simultaneous effects resolve in: errata, then card text, then general rules.
Value
The combat number on a card or fighter. Attack value is compared against defense value.
Versatile
A card that may be played as either an attack or a defense.
Zone
A coloured region of the battlefield. Ranged attacks and many effects care which zone a fighter is in.

Frequently asked questions

Common rulings that come up during a match.

General rules

Can I attack my own sidekicks, or attack my heroes with my own sidekicks?

No — you can never attack a friendly fighter.

Do you win combat against an attack card with a value of 0?

Yes. You win combat as long as you don't take damage.

If a defeated fighter is returned to the board, what health do they come back at?

Full health, unless the effect says otherwise.

Must card effects be resolved in the order printed?

Generally yes — but when a card offers multiple options to choose from, the player may choose the order.

What's the difference between "Move" and "Place"?

"Move" must respect the normal rules of movement (following lines, one space at a time). "Place" does not — it ignores those rules entirely.

If I gain an action, can I choose not to use it?

No. Actions cannot be skipped once gained.

Does "up to X spaces" include the option of moving 0 spaces?

Yes.

What's the difference between combat damage and effect damage?

Combat damage comes from comparing the attack card's value against the defense card's value (including any modifiers). Effect damage covers every other source.

Timing & rule changes

When does the game actually check for a winner?

Fighters are still defeated under the normal rules, but the game only checks whether there's a winner once AFTER COMBAT effects have finished resolving — not the instant the second-to-last hero falls. If both heroes are defeated at the same time, the active player wins.

What happens if a player has no fighters left on the board?

They become Dormant: they don't take actions, draw, or discard on their turn, their fighters can't take damage, and they can't be chosen as an opponent or targeted by "all opponents" effects.

Does "ignore the value" change a card's printed value?

No — it only disregards the value for combat damage purposes. The printed value is unchanged for any other effect that references it.

Maps & terrain

Are secret passage spaces considered adjacent?

No. You can move through a secret passage as if the spaces were adjacent, but they are not adjacent for the purposes of combat — no melee attacks across a passage.

Can the High Ground attack bonus stack?

No — you only get the bonus once, no matter how many High Ground spaces are involved.

Does High Ground apply to ranged attacks too, or only melee?

It only affects adjacent spaces in the direction the arrow points, regardless of attack type.
